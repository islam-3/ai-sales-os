## Read by hand, and a scan for what the metrics missed

Reading the transcripts turned up defects none of the metrics above
measure. Every reply in this run was then scanned for them (free,
read-only):

| Replies naming the business in full | 5/84 | 16/84 | 31/84 |

| Rest of the conversation (Sonnet extraction, Haiku classifier, coverage) | $0.0412 | $0.0381 | $0.0399 |

The script check passes the Russian word because it judges the
dominant script of a reply. The business-name count is against a
prompt that asks for roughly every third or fourth message: Sonnet
under-uses it and Luna overdoes it.

### Systematic or one-off

- **Sonnet, Arabic dialect: systematic.** 7/42 replies flagged in each
  run, all Levantine/colloquial forms (بتختلف، اللي، محتاجين، نقدر،
  يستاهل), and reading confirms them. One Chinese flag (配戴) besides.
- **Luna, repeated sentences: systematic as a behaviour.** It happened 3
  times, in 3 different languages, rather than as a repeat of one case.
- **Luna, hesitation reply too long: systematic.** Over the 0.6 guide on
  3/6 and 2/6.
- **Luna, consecutive replies of the same shape: systematic.** 33% and
  19% of pairs, against 8-17% for the other two arms in every run.
- **Luna, foreign word: one-off** (1 of 84 replies).
- **Terra, "costo" (Latin American) to a European Spanish visitor:
  one-off** (1 of 84 replies).
- **Hard checks: none failed in any arm or run.**

### Limits

- The dialect judge is itself Sonnet. It flagged Sonnet most, so it is
  not favouring its own replies, but its labelled-sample check
  (test-dialect-judge, LIVE) was not re-run for this.
- Two runs of six conversations is enough to see a systematic
  difference, not to put a precise rate on one.
- Terra's replies are a third shorter. Its answer to "how much does it
  cost?" in English is a single sentence with no question back. Whether
  that is focused or thin is a judgement for whoever reads the
  transcripts.
